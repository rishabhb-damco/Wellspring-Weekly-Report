Updates This Week

Account changes, launches, and initiatives — week of 25 Oct 2026
Live
New Landing Page Is Now Live
The redesigned Wellspring landing page went live this week. This is a key step toward improving post-click conversion — the previous page lacked dedicated therapy-service copy and a clear CTA above the fold. All Generic Search traffic is now directed to the new page. We will monitor quality score, bounce rate, and scheduling rate over the next 3–4 weeks to measure its impact on lead conversion.
Tracking
Early Signs from the New Landing Page — 3 Leads in 2 Days
Within just 2 days of the new landing page going live, 3 Google leads have been observed in the consultation sheet. Of these: 1 has not scheduled yet, 1 is in a pending stage — none have booked yet. This is a promising early signal — the volume in 2 days is encouraging — but it is too soon to draw conclusions on conversion impact. We will continue tracking scheduling rate and CPL over the coming weeks as the primary measures of landing page performance.
Live
Brand Search Campaign Launched
A Brand Search campaign (Damco_WS_Brand_Searc) has been launched to defend Wellspring's branded queries. The objective is straightforward: prevent competitors from bidding on and capturing traffic from people actively searching for "Wellspring" by name. Without a brand campaign, a competitor can outbid us on our own brand terms and intercept high-intent prospects who already know Wellspring. The campaign is currently in a bidding strategy learning phase and spending $0 — it is expected to ramp up and begin serving in the coming week. We will monitor impression share and cost once it exits learning.

Key Observations

CPL improved significantly to $54 — best result in 5 weeks. $268.85 spent on Generic Search alone, divided across 5 Google bucket leads, gives a CPL of $53.77. This is below the August benchmark of ~$99 and far below last week's $135. Context: DSA was paused, so total spend is lower than a typical week — but the CPL improvement is real. Generic Search is performing efficiently on its own.
Insurance eligibility is the persistent conversion blocker — 4 of 6 non-bookings were insurance-related. HAP (1), Medicaid ×2 (2), and Medicare (1) declines account for 67% of all non-bookings. This is a structural intake issue, not a campaign quality issue. The pipeline is delivering relevant enquiries, but eligibility is filtering out the majority. HAP resolution alone could lift scheduling rate by 10–15pp based on historical patterns.
Non-Google sources delivered well — 45% overall scheduling rate despite low Google rate. L1, L6 (external referral), L10 (internal, Lisa), and L12 (Psychology Today) all booked. External referrals and internal warm leads are converting at 60–100%. The account's blended scheduling performance is being sustained by non-paid channels. This underscores that intake quality and insurance coverage — not ad targeting — are the binding constraints on growth this week.
