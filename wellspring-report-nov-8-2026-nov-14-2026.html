Key Observations

Best efficiency week in months — CPL dropped to $41 on a consultation basis. Total spend fell 29% to $370 while platform conversions rose 75% to 7. The consultation-based CPL of $41 ($370 ÷ 9 Google leads) is the lowest recorded since tracking began in this format. 89% of Google leads booked an appointment — 8 of 9. The improvement is driven by two factors: DSA returning from its week-long absence (providing low-cost conversions at $19.66 CPL), and 8 of 9 Google leads being qualified adults or paediatric patients who were successfully scheduled.
DSA was completely absent the prior week (Nov 1–7) — a significant unexplained gap. Platform data confirms DSA ran 0 impressions and $0 spend for all 7 days of Nov 1–7, while Generic Search ran normally. DSA returned this week generating 2 conversions on $39 at a $19.66 CPL — the account's most efficient result. The prior week's higher CPL ($131 platform) is partly explained by DSA's absence; with DSA running, blended efficiency would have been materially better. The root cause must be investigated to prevent future gaps.
Child/teen patient pattern continues — 5 of 15 leads are paediatric. This is now the third consecutive week with a significant paediatric lead concentration. L5 (Theodore, ~6), L6 (Lilliana, ~13), L7 (Luke, ~4), L8 (Alice, ~8) all came from Google Ads and all scheduled successfully — suggesting Wellspring is accepting and scheduling these patients. If capacity exists for paediatric care, the child/teen keyword exposure is working. If not, negative keywords must be added immediately. This warrants a direct confirmation from Wellspring's scheduling team on whether child/teen clients are within current capacity.
