Key Observations
Negative keywords #28 and #29 (Medicare + addiction) are 18 days overdue and produced at least 3 wasted calls this week alone. Leads 1 (Medicare), 2 (Addiction+Medicaid), and 6 (Addiction/Psychology Today) all declined on categories that have been open actions since Sep 5. Adding Medicare, Medicaid, addiction-related, Cigna, and United Healthcare terms to both campaigns would have blocked several of these calls before click, reducing cost-per-qualified-lead significantly.
Sep 18 internal referrals: 4 leads, 4 booked (100%) — the best-performing channel this week and it costs nothing. Kendra, Melissa, and Lisa each referred patients directly from within the practice. Internal referrals require no ad spend and convert at 100%. This is worth tracking as its own source in the consultation sheet going forward. A week with strong internal volume looks very different from a pure-ads week, and separating the two gives a cleaner picture of what each channel actually produces.
CPL at $63 is the best in 4 weeks, but the Google scheduling rate dropped to 29% — these two facts are not contradictory. Higher lead volume (7 vs 4) drove CPL down while more non-qualifying traffic reached the call stage. Absolute bookings from Google stayed flat at 2. The lever that fixes both metrics simultaneously is negative keywords: fewer disqualified calls means higher scheduling rate AND lower cost per booked appointment, not just lower cost per enquiry.
