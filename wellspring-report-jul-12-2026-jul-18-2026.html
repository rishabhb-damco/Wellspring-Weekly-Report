Key Observations
DSA is back — both campaigns ran all 7 days. This is the first full week since late June with both campaigns active together. DSA averaged $3.43/click vs Broad Match’s $5.55 — 38% more efficient per click. DSA also generated 7 phone calls this week, which is where the highest-quality patient intent shows up. Keeping both running is the priority.
Medicaid blocked a lead for the 5th consecutive week — action needed now. Every week since mid-June, at least one Medicaid-insured person has contacted Wellspring and could not be seen. Adding “medicaid”, “state insurance”, and “government insurance” as negative keywords across both campaigns will stop these at the ad level before they reach the intake team.
Broad Match is hitting its $50/day cap every single day. Average daily spend was $63 — Google’s system is over-delivering because demand exceeds the budget limit. This means we are leaving qualified traffic on the table every day. Raising the Broad Match budget to $70–$80/day would directly unlock more clicks and likely 1–2 additional enquiries per week.
What’s Next
🚨 Immediate — Add Medicaid negative keywords
Add to both DSA and Broad Match: “medicaid”, “medicaid therapist”, “state insurance”, “government insurance”, “priority medicaid”. This is the 5th consecutive week this issue has appeared. It needs to be resolved before next week’s report.
🔍 Exploring additional platforms to scale leads
With Google Ads consistently delivering, the next step is evaluating Meta (Facebook & Instagram), Psychology Today premium listings, and YouTube to open additional patient acquisition channels and reduce reliance on search-only traffic.
