Key Observations
Broad Match is over budget most days — same pattern as last week. The $50/day cap was exceeded on 4 of 7 days (Sun, Mon, Tue, Fri), peaking at $87.56 on Tuesday — 1.75× the budget. This is now a two-week pattern: Google's bidding system is consistently finding more qualified searches than the budget allows. Raising the daily cap to $70–$80 would directly unlock more clicks and likely 1–2 more enquiries per week without changing anything else.
Two leads couldn’t be seen because Wellspring doesn’t offer medication management for minors. Both were for children under 18 — one via Google Ads, one via a PCP referral. The PCP referral can’t be controlled, but the Google Ads lead can be filtered before it reaches the intake team by adding “for adults” or “adult therapy” to Medication Management ad copy and headlines. Small copy tweak, saves intake capacity.
No Medicaid blocks this week — a positive development. For the past five consecutive weeks, at least one Medicaid-insured patient contacted Wellspring and couldn’t be seen. This week had none. Whether this is due to keyword changes, search pattern variation, or coincidence, it’s worth monitoring. If Medicaid leads reappear next week, adding negative keywords remains the recommended fix.
What’s Next
🔍 Raise Broad Match daily budget to $70–$80/day
The $50 cap has been over-delivering for two consecutive weeks. Raising it would directly capture more of the qualified search traffic Google is already finding. This is the single highest-impact change available right now.
✍ Add “for adults” to Medication Management ad copy
Two Medication Management for Minors leads this week added friction for the intake team. A simple copy update (“adult medication management”, “therapy for adults”) will reduce these at the source before they reach the front desk.
